# Chapter 7: Limitations and Threats to Validity

> Draft status: complete first draft. This chapter synthesizes the honest caveats already flagged in Chapters 3–6 into one place; nothing new is introduced. Outline source: `paper/thesis_outline.md` §7.

The aim of this chapter is to give the reader a single place to find what this thesis does *not* claim. Each section names a real limitation, briefly explains the evidence that bounds it, and points to the mitigation or future work that would close it. None of the limitations below changes the qualitative findings of the body chapters; their role is to keep the quantitative statements honest.

## 7.1 Simulator versus real multi-client system

All multi-client results in Chapter 6 — the disagreement decomposition (§6.3), the capacity-pressure sweep (§6.4), and the exact-optimality check (§6.5) — are produced on the simulator described in Appendix B. We did *not* build a real multi-client SSD serving stack, and we do not claim to have. The simulator's per-client SSD service curve is the closed form (3.9), calibrated from real single-client measurements (Chapter 5), so the absolute scale and shape of $\mu^{\mathrm{SSD}}$ are anchored in real execution. What the simulator necessarily abstracts away is the engineering layer: drafter/verifier pipelining, NCCL synchronization across clients, per-client KV-cache fragmentation, and batch-boundary effects that a real multi-tenant serving system would expose. We expect these to perturb the constants in our results but not the structural facts — the existence of an interior $k^\ast$ is a property of the modelling assumptions A1–A4 (Chapter 3), and the capped greedy's slack-regime advantage follows from $\mu^{\mathrm{SSD}}$ being single-peaked, neither of which is an artefact of the simulation harness. A real multi-client implementation is the largest single item in the future-work programme (§8.2) and the most direct route to falsifying or sharpening the present claims.

## 7.2 Single-drafter calibration

The timing model (3.3) was fit against measurements from a single target/drafter pair (Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-0.6B) on a single hardware setting (two RTX 4090 GPUs). This anchors the absolute scale of $a$, $b$, and $T_V$ but not the multi-client *heterogeneity* distribution of $b$. The experiments in Chapter 6 that depend on heterogeneous timing — particularly the $b_{\max}/b_{\min}$ scaling of strict-reversal share — are therefore performed on synthetic $b$-heterogeneity scans, not measured ones. Sensitivity scans across the calibrated $b$ neighbourhood show the main structural results remain in place; what we cannot demonstrate from this calibration alone is the *frequency* with which large $b$-heterogeneity arises in realistic deployments. Closing this gap requires measurements from at least one additional drafter family (e.g. Qwen3-1.7B or Llama3-1B) and ideally from a second hardware setting, both of which are deferred to future work (§8.x).

## 7.3 Robustness of assumptions A1–A4

The four modelling assumptions of §3.2 each carry their own uncertainty.

*A1 (power-law cache hit, $1 - p_{\mathrm{hit}}(F) = F^{-r}$)* is taken from Saguaro and is validated there for the single-client regime. It has not been independently re-validated under multi-client load, where contention effects could in principle alter the cache-hit elasticity. We treat A1 as the working hypothesis on which the rest of the analysis depends; a re-calibration of $r$ under multi-tenant load is part of the real-system future work.

*A2 (capped geometric fan-out)* invokes the Saguaro per-client optimal fan-out shape. Under shared verifier wall time, the *per-client* optimality argument no longer directly applies — the optimal shape may differ once budgets are coupled — and we adopt A2 as a tractable approximation. The synthetic $\phi_i$ scans of Chapter 6 use the same shape, so the comparison is internally consistent; whether a different shape would change the strict-reversal share is open.

*A3 (drafter timing $\mathrm{draft\_ms} = a k + b k B$)* is the most quantitatively supported of the four: the Alpaca fit (§5.5) returns $R^2 = 0.9966$ against `draft_detail_total_ms`. The fit was *not* successful against the aggregate `draft_total_ms`, which mixes service, build, populate, and communication overhead and produces an unphysical negative $b$. This is recorded in §5.5 as a lesson on the choice of timing target; users of the calibration must adopt the same target to reproduce $a$ and $b$.

*A4 ($T_V(k) = T_0 + \tau k$, linear in $k$)* is supported by the fit $T_V \approx 19.6 + 0.094 k$ ms with $R^2 = 0.87$ — adequate but the weakest fit in the calibration. The dependence on $B$ is small ($-0.002$ ms per unit) and we drop it in the proxy. Should a target/drafter pair appear in which $T_V$ is materially non-linear in $k$, A4 would need revision; the rest of the analysis would carry through with $T_V(k)$ replaced by the appropriate function.

Across all four assumptions, the mitigation is the same: the synthetic sensitivity scans in Chapter 5 hold the qualitative results (single-peakedness, $k^\ast$ ordering) stable across the parameter ranges probed. We do not claim the assumptions are *correct*; we claim that the body results follow from them and are robust to perturbations within the calibration neighbourhood.

## 7.4 Two-client scope of the closed-form structural claims

The closed-form structural results in Chapter 4 — Theorems 1 and 2, the FOC, and the KKT externality decomposition — are stated for the single-client and two-client settings. The $N$-client generalization is conjectural: it is supported by synthetic supporting evidence in §6.4 (the capacity-pressure sweep over $N \in \{2, 3, 5\}$, and the exact-optimality check over $N \in \{2, 3, 5, 8\}$), but we do not provide a closed-form proof that, e.g., the externality sign is preserved for general $N$. This is a deliberate scope decision. The two-client analysis is the smallest case in which the cross-client term is non-trivial, and it captures the structural mechanism; a clean $N$-client closed-form statement is future work (§8.x).

## 7.5 Conditional nature of the central theoretical results

Two of the body's theoretical claims are *conditional* on assumptions that we identify but do not prove unconditionally.

*Theorem 1 (single-peakedness)* requires Assumption 1, $q''(k) \ge 0$ on the effective interval $\mathcal{I}$. The miss probability $q(k) = G(k)(bk/(T_V - ak))^r$ contains the $\beta^k$ factor in $G$, whose second derivative is not sign-definite in closed form for finite $T_V$. We carry $q'' \ge 0$ as a regularity condition and validate it numerically on the calibrated grid (no observed violations). The large-$T_V$ asymptotic of Theorem 2 does *not* depend on Assumption 1, so the parameter monotonicities of the corollary remain unconditional even if Assumption 1 were to fail in some regime.

*The C4 exact-optimality result of §6.5* requires the per-client $\mu^{\mathrm{SSD}}$ to be discrete-concave on its feasible block. We verify discrete concavity at every grid point in the calibrated regime (228 of 228), but we do not prove that discrete concavity follows from the model. The non-concave counterexample of §6.5.2 — constructed by hand and *not* arising from the calibrated curve — shows the failure mode the conditionality is meant to exclude. Should a future calibration land outside the discrete-concave regime, the DP/MILP oracles of §6.5 remain valid and would expose the gap; the capped greedy would no longer be the certified optimum but would remain a low-cost heuristic with known performance bounds.

## 7.6 Objective scope: sum-throughput, not fairness or latency

The capped greedy F is a *sum-throughput* optimizer: at each step it consumes the unit with the largest marginal $\mu$, and stops when no positive-marginal unit remains. The proportional-fairness objective $\sum_i \log \mu_i$ is reported as a secondary diagnostic but is not the optimization target, and we make no claim that F is PF-optimal. Replacing $\mu$ with $\log \mu$ in Algorithm 1 produces a PF-targeted variant; we have not evaluated it. Likewise, an objective that *penalizes* idle capacity — resource utilization, tail latency, fair-share scheduling — would re-cast F's slack-regime "leave the budget idle" behaviour as a defect rather than an optimum. F is the right scheduler for the objective the thesis poses; whether it is the right scheduler for adjacent objectives is open.

## 7.7 Summary

The honest reading of the body is therefore: the structural results — non-monotone service, KKT externality, three-mode disagreement, slack-regime overcommit, exact optimality of the capped greedy under discrete concavity — hold within the modelling assumptions of Chapter 3 and the calibration of Chapter 5. The quantitative magnitudes — strict-reversal share, capacity-pressure gap percentages, $k^\ast$ value — are anchored to a single drafter/target pair on a single hardware setting and are robust within the synthetic sensitivity neighbourhood that Chapter 6 explores. What remains open and is owned by future work (§8) is generalization across $N$, across drafter families and hardware, across objectives beyond sum-throughput, and most importantly to a real multi-client serving stack in which the simulator's pipelining and KV-cache abstractions are replaced by measured engineering effects. We do not view any of these gaps as fatal; we view them as the natural next chapters of the programme this thesis has opened.
